# Invoices for Indian service firms: Rules

| Rule | Area | Text | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | money | Line taxable value is quantity times unit rate times (100 minus discount percent) divided by 100, worked in exact decimals with no floating point, and rounded half up to the paisa once, after the discount; money is stored as integer paise. | 3 units at Rs 1,333.33 with 12.50% discount → 3 x 1,333.33 = 3,999.99; less 12.5% = 3,499.99125; taxable Rs 3,499.99; 1.5 units at Rs 999.99, no discount → 1,499.985 rounds half up to Rs 1,499.99 (not 1,499.98); 0.333 units at Rs 100.00 → exactly Rs 33.30; a float multiply must not give 33.299999; 1 unit at Rs 5,000.00 with 100% discount → taxable Rs 0.00; allowed, tax 0.00 |
| R2 | tax | When place of supply is in the firm's own state, each line carries CGST and SGST, each equal to taxable value times rate divided by 200, rounded half up to the paisa on that line; no IGST. In a union territory without a legislature (Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Ladakh, Lakshadweep) the second half is UTGST, not SGST: the state master marks these territories, and invoices, registers and the e-invoice file name the head UTGST. | 10,000.00 taxable at 18%, firm and place of supply both Maharashtra → CGST Rs 900.00 and SGST Rs 900.00; 1,234.57 taxable at 18% → 1,234.57 x 18 / 200 = 111.1113; CGST Rs 111.11, SGST Rs 111.11; 333.33 taxable at 5% → 333.33 x 5 / 200 = 8.33325; CGST Rs 8.33, SGST Rs 8.33; 4,000.00 taxable at 0.25% → 4,000 x 0.25 / 200 = 5; CGST Rs 5.00, SGST Rs 5.00; 10,000.00 taxable at 18%, firm and place of supply both Chandigarh → CGST Rs 900.00 and UTGST Rs 900.00; no SGST line; the registers show the UTGST column |
| R3 | tax | When place of supply is in another state, each line carries IGST equal to taxable value times rate divided by 100, rounded half up to the paisa on that line, and no CGST or SGST. | 10,000.00 taxable at 18%, firm Maharashtra, place of supply Karnataka → IGST Rs 1,800.00; 1,234.57 taxable at 18% → 1,234.57 x 18 / 100 = 222.2226; IGST Rs 222.22; 333.33 taxable at 5% → 333.33 x 5 / 100 = 16.6665; IGST Rs 16.67 |
| R4 | tax | Known paisa differences: the same line can show CGST plus SGST one paisa above or below the IGST it would have carried, because each half is rounded on its own. This is accepted and never corrected by hand; the tax stored per line is the tax filed, and the page shows no hidden adjusting line. | 100.20 taxable at 5% → inter-state IGST Rs 5.01; intra-state CGST Rs 2.51 + SGST Rs 2.51 = Rs 5.02; difference +0.01 (an odd paisa cannot be halved, each half rounds up); 100.10 taxable at 5% → IGST 100.10 x 5 / 100 = 5.005 -> Rs 5.01; CGST Rs 2.50 + SGST Rs 2.50 = Rs 5.00; difference -0.01; 50.50 taxable at 18% → IGST Rs 9.09; CGST Rs 4.55 + SGST Rs 4.55 = Rs 9.10; difference +0.01; 1,000.00 taxable at 18% → IGST Rs 180.00; CGST 90.00 + SGST 90.00 = Rs 180.00; difference 0.00 (most lines match) |
| R5 | tax | The tax summary per rate and the invoice tax totals are the sum of the stored line tax. Tax is never recomputed from a total, and the server recomputes every line at Issue; a client total that differs by even one paisa is refused and the server value is shown. | 3 lines of 0.25 taxable at 18% (IGST) → each line 0.045 -> Rs 0.05; summary tax Rs 0.15, not Rs 0.14 from 0.75 x 18%; lines 333.33, 333.33, 333.34 at 5% intra-state → CGST per line 8.33, 8.33, 8.33; summary CGST Rs 24.99 and SGST Rs 24.99, not Rs 25.00 from 1,000.00 x 2.5%; browser sends total tax 15.01, server computes 15.00 at Issue → Issue is refused with the server figure 15.00; nothing is issued |
| R6 | money | Grand total is taxable values plus taxes, rounded half up to a whole rupee once for the whole document; the signed difference is stored and shown as one Round-off line. A paisa amount of exactly .50 rounds up. Foreign-currency invoices carry no round-off, neither in the foreign total nor in the INR total (the INR total is the sum of the INR lines, so the GST export value, the register and the portal all carry the same figure); our own plan invoices carry none either (R43). | taxable 1,000.41 plus IGST 18% = 180.07 → sum 1,180.48; grand total Rs 1,180.00; round-off -0.48; taxable 1,000.00 plus IGST 0.25% = 2.50 → sum 1,002.50; grand total Rs 1,003.00; round-off +0.50 (half up); taxable 3,499.99 plus IGST 630.00 → sum 4,129.99; grand total Rs 4,130.00; round-off +0.01; taxable 10,000.00 plus CGST 900.00 plus SGST 900.00 → sum 11,800.00; grand total Rs 11,800.00; round-off 0.00 is still stored and printed as 0.00; export invoice USD 1,250.00 at 83.4567 under LUT → INR total Rs 1,04,320.88 (1,250 x 83.4567 = 104,320.875 half up); round-off 0.00; this is the figure the register, the portal and the exchange gain test use |
| R7 | tax | A line rate must be one the product's dated rate table allows on the invoice date: 0, 0.25, 3, 5, 18 or 40 percent from 22-09-2025 (12 and 28 removed), 18 by default. Firms never edit rates. A rate that differs from the SAC's standard rate shows a visible warning, and a draft saved before a rate change is rechecked at Issue with old and new tax side by side and one click required. | service line at 12%, invoice dated 21-09-2025 → allowed, the table row for 12% ends 21-09-2025; same line, invoice dated 22-09-2025 → refused: 12% is not an allowed rate on 22-09-2025; choose 5, 18 or 40; draft saved 20-09-2025 at 12% on 10,000.00 taxable, issued 23-09-2025 → Issue screen shows old tax Rs 1,200.00 against the new rate's tax and will not issue until a new rate is chosen and the box ticked; SAC 998314 standard rate 18%, user types 5% → line shows the warning 'This invoice will go out at 5%, the usual rate is 18%'; issue still allowed |
| R8 | tax | Place of supply decides the tax split. A registered customer: the state of its GSTIN. An unregistered customer: the state of the address on record, else the firm's own state. It may be overridden with a reason only for immovable-property services (location of the property) and services performed in person (place of performance). Intermediary services follow one dated row in the tax table; today that row says they are supplied at the firm's location, so a foreign client is not an export, and it is unconfirmed until a chartered accountant has checked section 13(8)(b) of the IGST Act and its notification date (question CA1); changing the row needs no release. Place of supply is locked at Issue. | firm Maharashtra, customer GSTIN starts 29, 10,000.00 at 18% → place of supply Karnataka, IGST Rs 1,800.00; unregistered customer with address in Gujarat → place Gujarat, IGST Rs 1,800.00; unregistered customer, name only, state defaulted to the firm's state, no address → place of supply is the firm's state, CGST Rs 900.00 + SGST Rs 900.00; Karnataka recruitment agency acting as intermediary for a US client → place of supply Karnataka, not an export; CGST Rs 900.00 + SGST Rs 900.00; Maharashtra firm rents a service for a property in Goa, override with reason → place Goa, IGST Rs 1,800.00; override reason stored; draft shows 'Place of supply differs from customer state' |
| R9 | tax | An export of services is zero-rated only when the recipient is outside India, the place of supply is outside India and payment is in foreign exchange; a failed check blocks zero-rating with the reason. Under LUT: tax 0 on every line, the LUT number and the exact Rule 46 endorsement without payment of integrated tax. With payment of IGST: IGST at the normal rate on the INR taxable value, the endorsement ending ON PAYMENT OF INTEGRATED TAX, and a refund-tracking flag. The country of destination prints either way. SEZ supplies follow the same two paths. A foreign-currency invoice has no round-off (R6). | USD 1,250.00 at 83.4567 under LUT → taxable INR 1,04,320.88 (104,320.875 half up), tax 0.00, grand total Rs 1,04,320.88, no round-off (R6); same invoice with payment of IGST at 18% → IGST on INR taxable Rs 18,777.76; grand total Rs 1,23,098.64 (1,04,320.88 + 18,777.76), no round-off; flagged for refund tracking; export invoice whose place of supply is Maharashtra → blocked: 'Place of supply is in India, so this is not an export of services' ; no zero rating; export under LUT but no LUT recorded for the invoice date's FY → refused: record the LUT for FY 26-27 first (see R57) |
| R10 | money | Foreign-currency invoices store currency, the exchange rate with exactly up to 4 decimals (above 0), its source and the rate date. Each line's INR equivalent is foreign amount times rate rounded half up to the paisa, and INR totals are the sum of INR lines, so INR total can differ by paise from foreign total times rate. The rate is editable only on draft and locked at Issue; no rate for the date blocks Issue, a stale rate is never used silently. Foreign amounts keep 2 decimals (0 for currencies with none). | lines USD 5.55, 5.55, 5.55 at 83.4567 → INR per line 463.18, 463.18, 463.18; INR sum 1,389.54; USD 16.65 x 83.4567 = 1,389.55; difference -0.01; the sum of lines is filed; rate typed 83.45678 → refused: the rate has at most 4 decimals; rate typed 0 or -1 → refused: the rate must be above 0; USD invoice dated 26-11-2026 and the last stored USD rate is dated 20-11-2026 → prefilled rate shows its date; Issue is blocked until the user confirms it or types the rate for 26-11-2026 (see gaps G25) |
| R11 | money | A payment in foreign currency stores the foreign amount settled, INR credited to the bank, bank charges and the exchange gain or loss as four separate figures. Gain or loss = INR credited + bank charges + the typed platform fee converted to INR at the invoice's locked rate (half up) - the invoice's booked INR value of the foreign amount settled. The invoice settles at the foreign amount, and a platform fee is typed in the foreign currency, counts toward the foreign amount settled and is converted at the locked rate, so a fee never shows up as a false loss. The booked INR value is the INR invoice total (R6). | invoice USD 1,250.00 booked INR 104,320.88; bank credits INR 104,900.00 after charges of INR 350.00 → gain 929.12; invoice paid, balance USD 0.00; same invoice; bank credits INR 103,500.00, charges INR 400.00 → loss 420.88 (INR credited plus charges 103,900.00 is below the booked 104,320.88); invoice paid; customer sends USD 1,237.50 and the platform kept USD 12.50 as fee; invoice USD 1,250.00 booked INR 1,04,320.88 at 83.4567; bank credits INR 1,03,277.67; no bank charge → fee USD 12.50 x 83.4567 = 1,043.20875, INR 1,043.21; payment USD 1,237.50 plus fee USD 12.50 settles USD 1,250.00; gain = 1,03,277.67 + 0.00 + 1,043.21 - 1,04,320.88 = 0.00; balance 0.00 |
| R12 | law | E-invoicing applies to a firm when its aggregate turnover exceeded Rs 5,00,00,000 in any financial year from 2017-18 on; the flag, once true, cannot be turned off by the firm. Only B2B, export, SEZ and deemed-export documents get an IRN (also their credit and debit notes); B2C never calls the portal. The 30-day reporting block applies when the declared turnover is Rs 10,00,00,000 or more. The thresholds are dated settings owned by the product. For a firm that must have an IRN, Send on email and WhatsApp is shown as 'Waiting for IRN' until the IRN and signed QR are stored: the send is queued, the portal call is retried every 15 minutes until the 30-day limit, the message goes out by itself on success and the owner is told; a tax invoice without an IRN is never sent. | declared turnover Rs 5,00,00,000.00 in FY 2024-25 and below in all others → not required: the test is strictly above Rs 5 crore; declared turnover Rs 5,00,00,000.01 in FY 2024-25, Rs 80,00,000.00 in FY 2025-26 → e-invoice required now and for good, because one year crossed it; the 30-day block is off (below Rs 10 crore); declared turnover Rs 10,00,00,000.00 → e-invoice required and the 30-day block is on (10 crore or more); invoice to an unregistered customer for an e-invoice firm → shows 'e-invoice not applicable'; the portal is never called; e-invoice firm issues INV/26-27/00050 and the portal is down → Send shows Waiting for IRN; retries every 15 minutes; when the IRN arrives the email goes out with the signed QR and the owner is told; the invoice stays issued and payable meanwhile |
| R13 | time | For an e-invoice firm at Rs 10 crore or more, an IRN can be requested only while the days between the invoice date and the day of the request (IST calendar dates) are 30 or fewer; on day 31 generation is blocked with the reason and the document needs a credit note route. The daily job lists documents with 3 or fewer days left. Creating an e-invoice on a later date than the invoice date shows a warning. | invoice dated 01-03-2027, request on 31-03-2027 → 30 days apart: allowed; same invoice, request on 01-04-2027 → 31 days apart: blocked with 'older than 30 days'; same invoice on 28-03-2027 → 27 days apart, 3 days left: appears in the daily warning list; request at 23:59 IST on 31-03-2027, server clock UTC 18:29 → IST date 31-03-2027 is used, so still day 30 |
| R14 | time | An IRN can be cancelled only within 24 hours of the portal's acknowledgement time, with a coded reason; after that Cancel IRN is disabled and the screen says to issue a credit note. The firm's own filing status is not tested because the product cannot see it. A cancelled IRN's invoice number stays used and the invoice then needs a credit note or void by an owner. So the guards of Cancel IRN test only the 24 hours, no counted payment or credit note, and a coded reason; none says 'not in a filed return'. | IRN acknowledged 10:15 IST on 03-12-2026, cancel at 10:14 IST on 04-12-2026 → 23 h 59 m later: allowed; cancel at 10:16 IST on 04-12-2026 → 24 h 1 m later: refused, 'issue a credit note instead'; cancel at exactly 10:15:00 IST on 04-12-2026 → 24 h 0 m: allowed (24 hours inclusive) |
| R15 | limit | For a firm with e-invoicing on, portal limits apply on save and at Issue and win over the general limits: legal name 3 to 100 characters, address line 1 to 100, city 3 to 50, item description 3 to 300, HSN or SAC 4, 6 or 8 digits. A value saved earlier that is now too long blocks Issue with the exact field and limit. | firm legal name of 112 characters, e-invoice on → refused on save: 'Legal name: at most 100 characters for e-invoices, you typed 112'; customer city 'Pune' (4) and city of 55 characters → 'Pune' passes; 55 refused, limit 50; customer saved earlier with address line of 140 characters, invoice issued to a GSTIN customer → Issue blocked, field named, limit 100; item description of 2 characters → refused: 3 to 300 characters |
| R16 | tax | When the firm has a GSTIN every line needs an HSN or SAC of 4, 6 or 8 digits from the official SAC master; the minimum is 4 digits when the declared turnover of the preceding year is up to Rs 5,00,00,000.00 and 6 digits above it. Codes are text and keep leading zeros through import, export and JSON. An unknown code is refused with 'Not a valid SAC'. | turnover exactly Rs 5,00,00,000.00, line SAC 9983 → allowed (up to 5 crore: 4 digits); turnover Rs 5,00,00,000.01, line SAC 9983 → refused: 6 digits needed above Rs 5 crore, e.g. 998314; SAC typed 0998 then exported to CSV and imported back → stays 0998, never 998; SAC 99999999 not in the master → refused: Not a valid SAC; the picker offers search by words |
| R17 | tax | The document title follows the firm's registration and its line tax natures. Registered regular firm: Tax Invoice; taxable plus exempt or nil-rated lines: Invoice-cum-Bill of Supply; all exempt or nil-rated: Bill of Supply with the firm's GSTIN; composition firm: Bill of Supply reading 'Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies' and tax invoices blocked; unregistered firm: plain Invoice with no tax lines, no HSN or SAC and no tax columns. Each line stores its nature: taxable, nil-rated, exempt, non-GST or zero-rated. | regular firm, two taxable lines → title Tax Invoice; regular firm, one taxable line and one exempt line → title Invoice-cum-Bill of Supply; composition firm tries a Tax Invoice → blocked: only Bill of Supply with the composition sentence; unregistered firm; GSTIN 27... starts 01-08-2026; invoices dated 31-07-2026 and 01-08-2026 → 31-07-2026 prints Invoice with no tax; 01-08-2026 prints Tax Invoice; the old one is never reprinted differently; a Tax Invoice dated before the start date is blocked; unregistered firm adds a CGST line by hand → impossible: no tax line can exist |
| R18 | tax | A tax invoice to an unregistered recipient needs the recipient's name, address and state when the invoice taxable value is Rs 50,000.00 or more, and only the state below that. The test uses taxable value before tax. | unregistered customer, taxable value 49,999.99 → state only is enough; taxable value 50,000.00 → name, address and state all required; Issue lists what is missing; taxable 45,000.00 plus tax 8,100.00 = 53,100.00 total → state only: the test uses 45,000.00, not the total |
| R19 | tax | For a register, an inter-state invoice to an unregistered customer is tagged B2C large when its grand total is above Rs 1,00,000.00 and reported invoice-wise; every other unregistered invoice goes to the state-wise summary. The tag is stored on the document at Issue. | inter-state to unregistered, grand total 1,00,000.00 → B2C small; inter-state to unregistered, grand total 1,00,001.00 → B2C large; intra-state to unregistered, grand total 5,00,000.00 → B2C small: large applies only inter-state; registered customer, any amount → B2B, never B2C |
| R20 | tax | On a reverse-charge supply the invoice shows no tax, prints 'Tax payable on reverse charge: Yes' (and No on every other invoice) and a signature block, and the reverse-charge tax is not added to the total. An invoice cannot mix reverse-charge and normal lines. Legal services by an advocate to a registered business default to reverse charge. | advocate firm bills a GST-registered business 1,00,000.00 → tax 0.00, total Rs 1,00,000.00, 'Tax payable on reverse charge: Yes'; the customer owes 18,000.00 to the government; same firm adds a normal consulting line to that invoice → blocked: 'An invoice cannot mix reverse-charge and normal lines'; advocate bills an individual who has no GSTIN → default is normal tax: CGST Rs 9,000.00 + SGST Rs 9,000.00; the owner may switch to reverse charge; any ordinary consulting invoice → prints 'Tax payable on reverse charge: No' |
| R21 | money | Income-tax TDS on fees is suggested on the invoice taxable value before GST whenever GST is shown separately, never on the GST-inclusive total. The rate comes from the product's dated TDS table (section 393(1) of the Income-tax Act 2025 serial numbers with the old 194 numbers as aliases): Sl.6(iii) 10% professional fees, 2% technical fees, threshold Rs 50,000; Sl.6(i) contractors 1% individual or HUF and 2% others. The table row is chosen by payment date: from 01-04-2026 the new serial and Form 131, before it the old number and Form 16A. The user can type any rate or amount. | invoice taxable 50,000.00, CGST 4,500.00 + SGST 4,500.00, total 59,000.00, customer deducts 10% professional fees → TDS Rs 5,000.00 on 50,000.00, not 5,900.00; cash received Rs 54,000.00; balance 0.00; technical fees, taxable 1,23,456.78 at 2% → 1,23,456.78 x 2% = 2,469.1356 before rounding (see R23); contractor who is an individual, taxable 80,000.00 at 1% → TDS Rs 800.00; payment dated 31-03-2026 against payment dated 01-04-2026, same customer → 31-03-2026 shows '194J' and certificate Form 16A; 01-04-2026 shows '393(1) Sl.6(iii) (old 194J)' and Form 131; customer billed taxable 30,000.00 so far this FY in the section, new invoice 15,000.00 → total 45,000.00 is under 50,000: the row is prefilled and shows the hint 'TDS may not apply below Rs 50,000 a year' |
| R22 | money | A payment can carry two TDS types, income-tax TDS and GST TDS (2% of taxable value from government deductors: 1% central plus 1% state, or 2% IGST). Each shows on its own line, neither is money received, and both count toward settling the invoice. | government customer, taxable 3,00,000.00, GST 54,000.00, total 3,54,000.00; GST TDS 2% and income-tax TDS 10% → GST TDS Rs 6,000.00, income-tax TDS Rs 30,000.00; cash received Rs 3,18,000.00; balance 0.00; same invoice, only GST TDS recorded and cash 3,48,000.00 → balance 0.00; receipt lists GST TDS on its own line; GST TDS typed 7,000.00 where 2% is 6,000.00 → allowed (the figure is the deductor's); the screen shows the 2% suggestion beside it |
| R23 | money | The suggested TDS is rounded half up to the whole rupee (the deductor's usual practice; a chartered accountant must confirm) and always editable to the paisa. Cumulative income-tax TDS on an invoice, across all payment and allocation rows, cannot exceed 20 percent of its taxable value, the highest rate a deductor without a PAN pays (section 397(2)); an owner can raise it with a typed reason, and the owners are told whenever TDS is above the suggested rate. A payment of 0 that carries only TDS needs a certificate or challan reference. A residual of paise left by a different rounding is cleared by a write-off by an owner, or by an accountant up to Rs 10.00 per invoice with the reason 'rounding'; a balance under Rs 1.00 sends no reminder and shows no UPI QR, and offers one tap write-off; which needs an amount above 0 and at most the balance and a reason of 5 to 200 characters, and shows as write-off, not as a payment. | taxable 1,23,456.78 at 2% → exact 2,469.1356; suggested TDS Rs 2,469.00; invoice total Rs 1,45,679.00; customer deducts 2,469.00 and pays 1,43,209.86 → balance due 0.14; no reminder and no QR; the owner or accountant writes off 0.14 with the reason 'TDS rounding'; the invoice shows Written off, settled for reminders and ageing but counted as written off, not as collected; TDS typed 1,30,000.00 on taxable 1,23,456.78 → refused: 'TDS cannot be more than 20 percent of the invoice value before GST, Rs 24,691.36 (24,691.356 half up)'; amount, mode and TDS errors appear in one summary; write-off typed 0.00, or above balance, or reason of 3 characters → refused: amount must be above 0 and at most the balance; reason 5 to 200 characters; invoice taxable 50,000.00 plus GST 9,000.00; an entry of amount 0.00 with TDS 50,000.00 and no reference → refused: the cap is Rs 10,000.00 (20% of 50,000.00) and a TDS-only entry needs a certificate or challan reference; 10,000.00 with a reference is accepted, shows 'above the suggested 10%' and tells the owners; accountant writes off Rs 10.01 on one invoice, or Rs 10.00 with the reason 'discount' → refused for the accountant: up to Rs 10.00 and only with the reason 'rounding'; an owner may write off either |
| R24 | money | Balance due = grand total minus non-reversed payments minus TDS minus active credit notes minus customer credit applied to the invoice, never below 0. Status is computed from it: issued when nothing is paid or credited, partially_paid when it is above 0 and below the total, paid at exactly 0; draft and void are set only by Issue and Void. A fully credited invoice with no cash shows the label Credited, not Paid. A written-off invoice counts as settled for reminders, ageing and the balance, but not as collected in the collected-money reports. | total 11,800.00; cash 5,000.00 → balance Rs 6,800.00, partially_paid; then credit note 1,180.00 and customer credit applied 620.00 → balance Rs 5,000.00; total 11,800.00; payment of 5,900.00 later reversed → reversed payment is left out: balance 11,800.00, status issued; total 11,800.00; credit note 11,800.00 and no payment → balance 0.00, status paid, label Credited; 'collected this month' is not raised |
| R25 | money | A payment is recorded only on an issued or partially paid invoice. Amount is above 0 with 2 decimals, and amount plus TDS above the balance due is never refused for being too large: the person ticks 'keep the rest on account' and the rest becomes on-account money of the customer (R60); the tax receipt voucher for an advance is wave 2. The date is not before the issue date for money settling the invoice and not after today in IST; money that arrived before the invoice exists is recorded on account with its real receipt date (R60). Mode is cash, bank transfer, UPI, cheque, card or other; reference up to 60 characters is required for cheque, bank transfer and UPI. A possible duplicate needs one more tap: the same UTR or reference anywhere in the firm, or the same customer and amount within 3 days; the message names the earlier row, who entered it and on which invoice; reversed rows are ignored; every save carries a client key, so a retry or a double tap saves once. Only the reference and note can be edited afterwards. | balance 5,900.00, payment 5,900.00 → accepted; invoice paid; balance 5,900.00, payment 5,900.01 → refused unless the person ticks 'keep the rest on account': then Rs 5,900.00 settles the invoice and Rs 0.01 stays on account; balance 5,900.00, payment 5,400.00 with TDS 500.00 → accepted; paid; server clock 20:00 UTC on 26-11-2026, payment dated 27-11-2026 → accepted: 20:00 UTC is 01:30 IST on 27-11-2026, so 27-11-2026 is today; same payment typed again 2 hours later with the same reference → stopped with 'Looks like a duplicate of REC/26-27/00004' and a confirm button; UPI payment without a reference → refused: reference needed for UPI; Rs 50,000.00 recorded on 12-11-2026 by Ravi against INV/26-27/00013 with UTR 4421; the owner types Rs 50,000.00 for the same customer on 14-11-2026 against INV/26-27/00012 with no reference → stopped: 'Rs 50,000.00 already recorded on 12-11-2026 by Ravi on INV/26-27/00013' with one confirm button; the same UTR 4421 typed on any invoice is stopped the same way; a cash payment saved, undone within 10 seconds, then saved again with the same details → the undone row is ignored by the duplicate test and the second save goes through; a double tap with the same client key makes one payment |
| R26 | money | One payment can be split across invoices of the same customer. The default is oldest due date first, then oldest issue date, then lowest number. Allocation rows plus the on-account part must add up exactly to the payment amount (money above the open invoices stays on account of the customer, R60); the database refuses rows whose sum exceeds the payment. TDS is allocated per invoice on the same rows, so each invoice carries its own TDS and the rows' TDS adds up to the payment's TDS. | cash 10,000.00; invoice A due 01-12-2026 balance 6,000.00, invoice B due 05-12-2026 balance 7,000.00 → default allocation A 6,000.00, B 4,000.00; B left with 3,000.00; cash 10,800.00 and TDS 1,000.00; A taxable 6,000.00 total Rs 7,080.00, B taxable 4,000.00 total Rs 4,720.00, TDS 10% → A: cash 6,480.00 + TDS 600.00; B: cash 4,320.00 + TDS 400.00; both paid; rows add up to 10,000.01 on a payment of 10,000.00 → refused at save and by the database constraint; payment and credit note posted to the same invoice at the same instant → the invoice row is locked; together they cannot exceed the balance; the later one is refused with the new balance |
| R27 | money | A credit note can be made from an issued, partially paid, paid, credited or written-off invoice (never draft or void; the same list is used in the credit note object) and starts as a copy of the original lines; each line may be lowered but never above that line's remaining creditable taxable value (original minus credited by earlier active credit notes).
